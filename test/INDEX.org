#+TITLE: The tidal-viz harness

Probes and harnesses for the Tidal → Strudel visualiser.  The real code lives in
the project at =../daemon/= and =../tidal-viz.el=; the reasoning behind it is
written up in =../docs/design.md=.

* Does the playhead stay smooth across a mid-stream tempo change?
- =tempo-track.mjs= — spawns the daemon on a private port, feeds it a =/dirt/play=
  stream whose cycle clock is the integral of cps with one change at t0+6s (rise
  and drop), timestamps every stdout frame line, and reports the slopes,
  monotonicity and the largest step.  It found a real bug: the playhead JUMPED in
  a single frame -- backwards on a rise, forwards on a drop -- by
  =(cpsB-cpsA)*(b-0.27)= cycles, where =b= is how long after the switch the next
  event lands (0.114..0.169 cycles for a 0.575->1.2 change).  The cause was
  anchoring the clock on the newest event, which Tidal sends ~0.27s in the
  FUTURE: the step was a fixed time converted to cycles at the current cps, so it
  changed size the instant a new-cps event arrived.  The daemon now anchors on the
  newest event that has already been REACHED and extrapolates forward.  Re-running
  this probe after that change reports *monotone* with a worst step of 0.0445
  cycles, and the drop's forward leap of +0.1319 becomes one small backward step
  of -0.0121.  What remains is a sawtooth, not a leap: between events the clock
  must extrapolate with the last cps it heard, so for up to one event gap it runs
  at the old tempo and then snaps by about =gap * dcps= (~0.03-0.045 cycles).  Only
  Tidal could remove that, by announcing a tempo change sooner than the events
  embody it.  Keep this probe -- it is what would catch a regression.
  NOTE: its "law check" and "formula check" lines hard-code the PRE-FIX formula,
  so after the fix those two numbers no longer describe the daemon.  Read the
  slopes, the monotonicity line and the worst-step line.
  =--visual-latency L= subtracts exactly =L*cps=.
  Run =node tempo-track.mjs [rise|drop|latency|phase|all]=; evidence in =data/=.
  Its own trap: the bundle timetag must be WALL clock -- feeding it =mono()= made
  the daemon draw one frame and go stale.

* What does @strudel/draw actually offer?
- =punchcard-calib.mjs= — calibrates =getPunchcardPainter= by putting one block at
  a known cycle and reading which x-pixels it lands on.  The answer is that it is a
  thin wrapper around =__pianoroll= and renders byte-identically, and that its 4th
  argument is a =[lookbehind, lookahead]= tuple rather than =cycles=/=playhead= —
  passing the latter to the factory is silently ignored.  Run from the test directory:
  =node --import ../daemon/register.mjs punchcard-calib.mjs=.

* Are the style variants really different?
- =smear-trajectory.mjs= — does =smear= alone leave a trail, or saturate the
  canvas?  It leaves none and saturates: =smear= is only truthiness-tested, so
  0.2/0.5/0.8 are byte-identical, and with no decay term ink climbs to 94% -- a
  solid block.  This is why =trails= needs a fading wash rather than a smear value,
  and the sweep that picked =fade: 0.6= is here.
- =styles-verify.mjs= — renders every style from the *same* haps and reports ink%
  and distinct colours side by side, with one distinguishing statistic each
  (playhead orientation, ink bounding box).  It extracts =STYLES= from =daemon.mjs=
  itself, so it cannot drift from what actually ships.
- =e2e-check.sh= — drives the real daemon over the real socket and stdin control
  channel for three style combinations, with outputs under =e2e/=.  Documents a
  harness gotcha that cost a run: =... | node ... &= makes =$!= the wrapper
  *subshell*, so killing it orphans the daemon, which then squats on 57121 and makes
  every later case fail as a *silent no-op* -- no frames, no error.  Use =exec= in
  the subshell, and check the port before each case.
- =synth-tidal.mjs= — the synthetic Tidal sender =e2e-check.sh= drives: a
  =/dirt/play= stream shaped like Tidal's, one event per packet with a bundle
  timetag ~270ms out, which takes ghci, Tidal, SuperDirt and scsynth out of the
  picture so that anything slow is the daemon's own code.
